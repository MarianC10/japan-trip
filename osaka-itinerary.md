# Osaka — 2-Day Itinerary

The goal is *not to see absolutely everything*, but to cover the parts of Osaka that feel most distinctive while leaving enough room to wander, eat, and slow down.

## Day 1 — Southern Osaka: temples, retro streets & Namba

### Morning — Sumiyoshi Taisha

* *Sumiyoshi Taisha*
* Allow roughly *1–1.5 hours*
* Go relatively early so you're not starting the day in the busiest part of Osaka.

### Late morning — Shitennō-ji

* *Shitennō-ji*
* Allow roughly *1–1.5 hours*
* One of Osaka's most historically important temples.
* Worth visiting if you want some traditional Japanese atmosphere alongside the very urban Osaka experience.

### Afternoon — Den Den Town → Namba

* *Den Den Town*
* Walk toward *Namba*
* Browse shops and side streets rather than trying to visit individual stores systematically.
* If you're not particularly interested in anime, manga, gaming or electronics, this can be shortened.

### Late afternoon / evening — Namba & Minami

Walk through:

* *Namba*
* *Hozenji*
* *Shinsaibashi*
* *Amerikamura*
* *Dotonbori*

Have *Rikuro's* somewhere along the way rather than making it a dedicated stop.

### Evening — Shinsekai

* Head to *Shinsekai*
* Walk around the retro streets
* *Tsutenkaku — go up this time*
* Have dinner/drinks in the area if the atmosphere appeals to you.

*This is intentionally a flexible evening.* If Dotonbori is keeping you entertained, there's no need to rush to Shinsekai at a particular hour.

## Day 2 — Osaka Castle, central Osaka & Umeda

### Morning — Osaka Castle

* *Osaka Castle Park*
* *Osaka Castle*
* If you want a museum component, visit the castle museum while you're there.

Don't spend the entire morning inside museums; the park and exterior views are a significant part of the experience.

### Optional museum — Osaka Museum of History

If you enjoy history, this is the museum I'd prioritize.

It pairs particularly well with Osaka Castle because it gives you context about Osaka's development and has excellent views toward the castle.

*Allow:* ~1.5–2 hours

If museums aren't a major interest, *skip it* and use the time for wandering/eating instead.

### Afternoon — Central Osaka → Umeda

Keep this part deliberately loose.

You can:

* Explore *Honmachi / central Osaka*
* Stop for lunch
* Head toward *Umeda*
* See the modern skyline

### Sunset / evening — Umeda Sky Building

* *Umeda Sky Building*
* Go up for sunset if possible.
* This is the *one skyscraper observation deck I'd definitely prioritize*.

You can see *Harukas 300* from around the city without needing to go up it. There's little benefit in spending limited time visiting multiple observation decks.

### Optional evening

If you still have energy:

* *teamLab Botanical Garden Osaka*

Treat this as a *bonus rather than a commitment*. If you're tired after two full days, skip it without feeling like you're missing something essential.

# What we're deliberately leaving out

### ❌ Osaka Aquarium Kaiyukan

Dropped for now.

### ❌ Katsuoji

Too much travel for a two-day Osaka itinerary.

### ❌ Hoshi no Buranko

Also save this for a longer/nature-focused visit.

### ❌ Harukas 300

See the building/skyline, but don't bother going up when you're already doing Umeda Sky Building.

### ❌ Tempozan Ferris Wheel

Not enough priority once the aquarium is gone.

### ❌ Osaka Dojimahama Tower

Nice building, but not worth dedicating time to.

### 🟡 teamLab Botanical Garden

Optional evening bonus.

### 🟡 Den Den Town

Included, but flexible. Skip or shorten it if anime/gaming/electronics aren't particularly interesting to you.

### 🟡 A Happy Pancake Shinsaibashi

Don't build your itinerary around it. Have it if you're nearby and want pancakes.

## Why I like this version

You're getting *three very different sides of Osaka*:

*Day 1:* traditional Osaka → retro Osaka → neon/food/nightlife Osaka

*Day 2:* historic Osaka → modern central Osaka → futuristic skyline Osaka

And importantly, there are *no major cross-city detours* during the core of either day. Sumiyoshi Taisha is the one early detour, but putting it first makes it manageable
